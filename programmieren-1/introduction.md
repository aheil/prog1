# Introduction

## Programming

Why do we program computers in the first place? The answer is simple: we want computers to do something for us, whether that is calculating values, drawing images, or processing text. In most cases, we develop software that enables other people, who may not be able to program themselves, to perform tasks on a computer more easily and efficiently.

Ideally, we would simply tell a computer in natural language what we want it to do and receive the desired result. To a certain extent, AI systems such as ChatGPT, Copilot, and Gemini already make this possible. However, natural language is inherently vague and, from a computer's perspective, often imprecise. The output generated by such AI systems depends on the data they were trained on, the way they interpret our instructions, and a certain degree of randomness.

To evaluate, verify, and, when necessary, adapt the results produced by AI systems as they exist today, it is essential not only to understand the fundamentals of programming, but also to be able to apply them in practice. Programming skills provide the knowledge required to assess whether a proposed solution is correct, efficient, secure, and maintainable. They enable us to recognize errors, understand the limitations of AI-generated code, and design robust solutions for complex real-world problems.
